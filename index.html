The 10 things that matter most

  1. Next deadline: the 2025 Form 11, the 2025 balance and 2026 preliminary tax are due by Nov 18, 2026 if you both file and pay on ROS.
  2. You are taxed on profit, not takings. Every genuine business cost you record cuts your bill by 26% to 47% of its value for most drivers.
  3. Declare every fare and tip. Taxi apps and card payment companies already report your income to Revenue.
  4. Your taxi gets a 40% yearly write-off of its remaining value, with no cost cap. Ordinary business cars get 12.5% on at most €24,000.
  5. Work out your private-use % from a mileage log and apply it consistently. It is the number Revenue checks most.
  6. Pension contributions are the biggest relief (up to 40% back), and you can backdate a contribution to last year until the filing deadline.
  7. Claim your personal credits: Earned Income Credit, Rent Tax Credit, health expenses, home carer or single parent credits.
  8. Keep your tax clearance live. No clearance means no SPSV licence renewal.
  9. Move 15% of every week's takings into a separate Tax pot. October will never be a shock.
  10. Keep records for six years and follow the 2-minutes-a-shift routine in Part 7.

Part 1The basics: how a taxi driver is taxed

As a self-employed taxi driver you pay three charges on your profit, not your takings: income tax, USC and PRSI. All three are worked out and paid through one yearly tax return, called Form 11.

The one formula to remember

Takings − business expenses − capital allowances = taxable profit

  • Takings = every euro passengers pay you: cash, card, app fares (the full fare, before the app's commission) and tips. Tips are taxable.
  • Business expenses = running costs like fuel, insurance, repairs, app commission (Part 4).
  • Capital allowances = the tax write-off for the car itself and its taxi equipment (Deep dive on your car).

You pay tax only on what is left. Every legitimate expense you record lowers that number.

Taxi fares and VAT

Passenger transport is exempt from VAT in Ireland. You do not charge VAT, normally do not register for it (but see the app commission trap in Part 4), and cannot claim VAT back. So you record your costs at the full price you paid, VAT included.

Credits versus deductions

  • A tax credit cuts your tax bill euro for euro. A €2,000 credit means €2,000 less tax.
  • A deduction (an expense or allowance) cuts your profit. It saves you your top rate of tax, USC and PRSI on that amount.

So the value of every receipt depends on how high your profit is.

Part 2Getting set up (do this once)

You need four things in place: an income tax registration, ROS access, a valid tax clearance certificate, and a separate bank account. Tax clearance matters most to you as a taxi driver: the NTA will not renew your SPSV vehicle licence without it.

This guide assumes you own or lease your taxi and work for yourself (a sole trader). If a company employs you on a payslip, you are a PAYE worker and most of this guide does not apply.

  1. Register for income tax self-assessment. Use the eRegistration service in myAccount and complete Parts A and B of Form TR1. Self-employed people must register. (Revenue: who should register)
    • Exception: if you have a PAYE job and the taxi is a small sideline, you can declare it on a Form 12 in myAccount instead, as long as your taxable non-PAYE income is €5,000 or less and gross non-PAYE income is under €30,000.
  2. Get ROS access. Revenue Online Service (ROS) is where you file Form 11 and pay. Most self-employed people must file online. Filing and paying through ROS also gives you a later deadline each November (Part 3).
  3. Keep tax clearance live all year. Apply for eTax Clearance through ROS or myAccount. When you book your SPSV renewal, the NTA checks your tax status with Revenue using your PPSN. (NTA: renewing an SPSV licence)
    • Get it done at least 48 hours before you book your renewal inspection.
    • The name on your tax clearance must match your licence exactly ("James Murphy", not "Jim Murphy"). (NTA Guide G2)
    • Clearance needs all returns filed and all tax paid (or a payment arrangement agreed with Revenue). A late return can cost you your licence renewal, not just a penalty.
  4. Open a separate bank account for the taxi. Not legally required, but it is the single biggest time-saver. Point your card terminal and app payouts into it, and pay taxi costs from it. Your bank statement then becomes half your bookkeeping.
  5. Use a calendar-year accounting period (1 January to 31 December). It matches the tax year and keeps the rules simple.
  6. Decide: accountant or do-it-yourself. Many drivers file their own Form 11. If you use an accountant, their fee for preparing your business accounts and return is a deductible expense.

Your first year in business

  • You usually do not have to pay preliminary tax in your first year, because the "100% of last year" option is normally nil. (Revenue: preliminary tax)
  • Your first big bill comes the following October, when you pay year one's tax and year two's preliminary tax together. Start setting money aside from day one (Part 7).
  • Costs you paid in the 3 years before you started (for example the SPSV driver test, Area Knowledge test and licence fees) can normally be claimed as pre-trading expenses in your first year.

Part 3The tax calendar: one big deadline a year

Your whole tax year comes down to one date: 31 October, or a later date in mid-November if you both file and pay on ROS. On that day you do three things at once: file last year's Form 11, pay last year's balance, and pay this year's preliminary tax.

Act now if your 2025 return is not done. The ROS deadline for the 2025 Form 11, the 2025 balance and 2026 preliminary tax is Nov 18, 2026. It only applies if you both file and pay through ROS; otherwise the deadline was 31 October 2026. A pension contribution paid by that date can still cut your 2025 bill (Deep dive on pensions). (Revenue eBrief 034/26)

In your first year in business there is usually no preliminary tax, so the first deadline only settles year one. From then on, every autumn bill covers two years.

Preliminary tax in plain words

Preliminary tax is a down-payment on the current year's tax, due by 31 October of that same year. It covers income tax, USC and PRSI. You must pay at least the lowest of these three: (Revenue: preliminary tax)

Option How much Best when
A 90% of this year's final tax Your income has dropped this year
B 100% of last year's tax Your income is steady or rising (the safe default)
C 105% of the tax for the year before last, paid by monthly direct debit You want small monthly payments instead of one big October bill

Worked example. Your 2026 tax comes to €6,428 (Part 1). In October 2027 you pay the 2026 balance (€6,428 minus whatever preliminary tax you paid in November 2026), plus 2027 preliminary tax of at least €6,428 under option B. That first "double" October is why you must save from the start.

Tip for taxi drivers: option C spreads the bill over the year by direct debit, which suits a weekly cash-flow business. Set it up on ROS under My Services.

What happens if you are late

  • Late filing surcharge: 5% of your tax (capped at €12,695) if you file within two months of the deadline; 10% (capped at €63,485) after that. (Revenue TDM 47-06-08)
  • Interest on late payment: charged for every day the tax is late, at roughly 8% a year.
  • Lost tax clearance: this blocks your SPSV vehicle licence renewal, which can stop you working.
  • Can't pay in full? File on time anyway and contact Revenue about a phased payment arrangement. Filing late makes everything worse.

Part 4Every expense you can deduct

You can deduct any cost spent wholly and exclusively for the taxi business. Where a cost is partly personal (the car, your phone), you deduct only the business share. Big items that last years (the car, the meter) are not expenses; they get capital allowances instead (next section).

Every cost below needs evidence: a receipt, invoice or statement. No receipt, no claim.

The private-use percentage is the number Revenue looks at most closely. If the taxi is also your family car, claiming 100% business use is a red flag. Work the figure out from a mileage log (Part 7), write it down, and apply the same percentage to every car cost and to the capital allowance.

A VAT trap on app commissions

Taxi fares are VAT-exempt, but the booking service an app sells you is not. If your app's commission invoice comes from a company based outside Ireland, the reverse-charge rule can require you to register for VAT and pay Irish VAT on that commission yourself, with no way to reclaim it. The same applies to other services you buy from abroad. Check who issues your app invoices and where they are based; if it is a foreign company, ask an accountant or Revenue whether you need to register. (Revenue: VAT treatment of taxi drivers)

Deep diveYour car: the biggest tax write-off you have

A licensed taxi gets one of the most generous allowances in Irish tax: 40% of its remaining value written off every year, with no cost cap. An ordinary business car gets only 12.5% a year on a maximum of €24,000. (Revenue TDM 11-00-04: Capital allowances for taxis)

Does your car qualify for the 40% rate?

How the 40% works

Each year you claim 40% of the car's tax written-down value (what is left after earlier claims). The claim is biggest in year one and shrinks each year. You get a full year's allowance in the year you buy, even if you buy late in the year, as long as the car is in use on 31 December.

The taxi's allowance shrinks every year, but it beats the ordinary-car rate for the first four years and writes off almost the whole cost within eight.

If you also use the taxi privately, the value still drops by the full 40% each year, but you claim only the business share. Example: a €40,000 taxi with 90% business use gives a year-one claim of €16,000 × 90% = €14,400.

Other equipment

Item Tax treatment
Roof sign / taxi plate 12.5% a year over 8 years
Meter, card terminal, dashcam, phone handset 12.5% a year over 8 years
Home EV charger (business share) 12.5% a year over 8 years, on the cost after any SEAI grant
Taxi licence itself No allowance (only an old scheme for licences bought before 21 November 2000)
Small items under about €100 (cables, mounts) Normally just claimed as an expense

Buy, hire purchase, lease or rent?

How you get the car What you claim
Buy outright 40% capital allowance on the full cost
Hire purchase 40% capital allowance on the full cost once the car is in use, plus the interest as an expense
Lease The lease payments as an expense (the 40% rate is not available to you or the leasing company)
Rent a taxi from another licence holder The rental payments as an expense

All of these are reduced by your private-use percentage.

Grants reduce what you can claim

The NTA's eSPSV grant helps taxi owners buy electric vehicles; the 2026 scheme opened on 25 February 2026 with €7 million, first come first served. You must hold a provisional grant offer before you buy, and keep the car licensed as an SPSV for 3 years or repay part of the grant. (NTA eSPSV 2026, eSPSV26 FAQs)

For tax, the grant is not income, but you can only claim capital allowances on the part of the cost you paid. Example: a €45,000 electric taxi with a €15,000 grant → claim the 40% on €30,000.

When you sell or trade in the car

Compare the sale or trade-in price with the car's tax written-down value:

  • Sold for less → you get an extra one-off deduction (a balancing allowance).
  • Sold for more → the difference is added back to your profit (a balancing charge).

Keep the sale or trade-in invoice; it goes on your return that year.

Legal timing tip

Because the year you buy gets a full 40%, buying your replacement taxi in December rather than January brings a large deduction forward by a whole year. Only do this if you genuinely need the car; never buy just to save tax.

Family planning that is fully legal

  • Joint assessment. Married couples and civil partners can be taxed together. The higher earner gets a bigger 20% band and the full married personal credit, which is worth €2,000 or more a year where one partner earns little.
  • Employing your spouse or partner. If your partner genuinely works in the business (bookings, bookkeeping, answering the phone), you can pay them a fair wage for the hours worked. The wage is a deductible expense, and they use their own credits and bands. It must be real work at a realistic rate, run through a payroll registration with Revenue. Get advice before starting; this is an area Revenue checks.
  • Using the second 20% band. Two-income married couples can use up to €35,000 more at 20% (€37,500 in 2027), capped at the lower earner's income.

Tax-free income worth knowing about

  • Rent-a-room relief: rent from a lodger in your own home is tax-free up to €14,000 a year (€16,000 from 2027).
  • Selling solar electricity to the grid: up to €400 a year tax-free (€600 from 2027).

Reliefs that do NOT apply to you

These are for employees only, so don't claim them: flat-rate expenses, remote working relief, the bike-to-work scheme and the TaxSaver commuter ticket.

Your PRSI also buys you benefits

Class S PRSI entitles you to the State Pension (Contributory), Jobseeker's Benefit for the Self-Employed, Treatment Benefit (dental and optical), Invalidity Pension, Maternity, Paternity and Parent's Benefit, and more. Keep paying it on time so your record stays complete. (gov.ie: Class S benefits)

Deep divePension contributions: the biggest legal tax saver

Paying into your own pension is the single most powerful tax relief available to a self-employed driver. Every €100 you put in costs you only €60 if your profit is in the 40% band, or €80 in the 20% band. And you can decide how much to pay after the year ends, once you know your profit.

How much you can put in with tax relief

The limit is a percentage of your taxi profit (your "net relevant earnings"), based on your age, on profit up to €115,000:

Your age during the tax year Maximum % of profit
Under 30 15%
30 to 39 20%
40 to 49 25%
50 to 54 30%
55 to 59 35%
60 and over 40%

Source: Zurich: pension contributions explained. Anything paid above the limit is not lost; the relief carries forward to later years.

What the relief covers

  • Income tax: yes, at your top rate (20% or 40%).
  • USC and PRSI: no. You still pay these on the full profit.
  • Growth inside the fund: tax-free.
  • At retirement (from 60): up to 25% of the fund tax-free (lifetime cap €200,000); the rest is taxed as income when you draw it.

The backdating trick (completely legal)

A lump sum paid after 31 December, but by the tax return deadline, can be treated as paid in the previous year. You make the election on the Form 11.

  • Right now: a contribution paid by Nov 18, 2026 can still reduce your 2025 tax bill, if you file and pay through ROS by that date.
  • Every year after: contribute for year X by 31 October of year X + 1 (or the ROS date in November).
  • Backdated relief also lowers the preliminary tax you owe for the current year.

Worked example (2026, single, age 45)

Taxi profit: €55,000. Age limit: 25% × €55,000 = €13,750 maximum.

Pension paid Income tax saved Real cost to you
€0 €0 €0
€5,000 €2,000 (all at 40%) €3,000
€11,000 €4,400 (brings profit down to the €44,000 band edge) €6,600
€13,750 (the maximum) €4,950 (€11,000 at 40% + €2,750 at 20%) €8,800

The sweet spot is usually the amount that brings your taxable profit down to the top of the 20% band (€44,000 in 2026, €46,500 in 2027). Every euro above that line gets 40% relief.

Practical steps

  1. In September or October, estimate last year's profit from your records.
  2. Work out the amount that brings your profit down to the 20% band edge, within your age limit.
  3. Pay it into a PRSA or personal pension before the deadline (a financial adviser or your bank can set one up; compare charges).
  4. Keep the pension provider's receipt and tick the backdating election on your Form 11.
  5. Consider a small monthly direct debit too, so you are not finding a lump sum every October.

Part 6Planning, avoidance and evasion: where the lines are

There are three very different things people call "saving tax". Only the first is safe. Everything recommended in this guide is tax planning: using reliefs the law deliberately gives you.

Tax planning Tax avoidance Tax evasion
What it is Using reliefs exactly as the law intends Artificial arrangements whose main purpose is a tax saving Hiding income or inventing costs
Legal? Yes, and expected Technically legal-looking, but Revenue can cancel it No, it is a crime
Taxi examples Claiming every real expense; the 40% taxi allowance; pension contributions; joint assessment; preliminary tax by direct debit; replacing the car in December Paying a spouse a wage for work they don't do; a scheme that promises "tax-free" earnings; inflating the business-use percentage Not declaring cash fares or tips; claiming family car costs as 100% business; fake or borrowed receipts
What happens if Revenue finds it Nothing; you were right The saving is cancelled under the general anti-avoidance rule, plus a surcharge and interest Back tax, interest, penalties up to 100% of the tax, possible publication in the defaulters list and prosecution

The test for any idea: would I happily explain this to a Revenue auditor, with the paperwork in front of me? If yes, it is planning.

What Revenue already knows about your takings

Assume Revenue can see most of your income before you declare it:

  • Taxi apps report you every January. Under the EU DAC7 rules, platforms report each driver's name, address, tax number, date of birth, bank account, number of trips per quarter, payouts and commissions. (Uber DAC7 FAQ, Revenue: DAC7)
  • Card payment companies report too. Merchant acquirers make returns of card payment transactions to Revenue under section 891D of the Taxes Act. (2012 Regulations)
  • The NTA and Revenue are linked. Your SPSV licence is tied to your PPSN and tax clearance.
  • Bank, property and vehicle records let Revenue spot a lifestyle that doesn't match declared profit.

So your declared takings must be at least your app payouts plus card settlements plus a realistic cash figure. A driver declaring less than the apps and card companies report will be flagged automatically.

Made a mistake? Fix it yourself first

  • Spotted an error on a return you already filed? You can usually amend it in ROS. Correcting it yourself, before Revenue contacts you, is treated far more leniently.
  • Bigger problem (for example, past cash not declared)? Make an unprompted qualifying disclosure: a written statement of the tax and interest owed, paid at the same time or by an agreed instalment plan. It brings the lowest penalties, and qualifying disclosures are not published. (Revenue: qualifying disclosure)
  • How far back? Revenue can normally review the last 4 years, but there is no time limit where there is fraud or neglect.
  • Get an accountant or tax adviser for any disclosure; the wording matters.

Paper or phone?

  • Photograph everything as a backup, and keep the paper originals in one envelope or box per month.
  • Revenue's Receipts Tracker in myAccount and ROS lets you store digital copies of receipts with Revenue itself.
  • Fuel receipts fade: photograph them the same day.
  • Paying by card from the taxi account means the bank statement backs up any lost receipt (but a receipt is still the stronger proof).

When to keep records longer than six years

  • Car purchase and sale documents: keep them for six years after the year you sell the car, because they support every year's capital allowance.
  • Anything relating to a year under Revenue review or appeal: keep until it is closed.

Part 9Filing Form 11 step by step

If your monthly summary uses the categories below, filling in Form 11 is mostly copying 12-month totals into the right boxes. ROS does the tax calculation for you.

Your expense categories, matched to the Form 11 boxes

Use these categories in your monthly summary all year:

Your category What goes in it Form 11 box (Extracts from Accounts)
Takings Cash fares, card fares, app fares before commission, all tips Sales / Receipts / Turnover
Motor Fuel or charging, insurance, servicing, repairs, tyres, NCT, motor tax, tolls, parking, valeting Motor, Travel and Subsistence
Accountant Accountant and bookkeeping fees Consultancy and Professional Fees
Wages A genuine wage paid to a spouse or helper through payroll Salaries / Wages
Equipment repairs Repairs to the meter, roof sign or card terminal Repairs / Renewals

Leave Depreciation at zero; the capital allowance replaces it. Every figure is the 12-month total for the year. (Revenue TDM 38-01-04J: Extracts from Accounts)

The filing steps

  1. Gather your numbers. Year totals for each category, business-use %, capital allowance working, pension certificate, medical and rent receipts.
  2. Sign in to ROS and open the pre-filled Form 11 for the year. Check your ROS certificate has not expired well before the deadline. (Revenue: key steps)
  3. Self-employed income panel. Describe the trade as "taxi operator", give the accounting period (1 January to 31 December) and enter the Extracts from Accounts using the table above.
  4. Adjustments. Add back the private share of motor and phone costs.
  5. Capital allowances. Enter the wear and tear allowance on the taxi and equipment; enter any balancing allowance or charge if you sold a car.
  6. Tax credits and reliefs. Claim the Earned Income Credit, Rent Tax Credit, health expenses, home carer or single parent credits, and your pension contribution. Tick the box to treat a pension contribution as paid in the previous year if you are backdating it.
  7. Clear every yellow tick. Pre-filled panels carry a yellow tick that must turn white before you can submit.
  8. Self-assessment panel. Review ROS's calculation of income tax, USC and PRSI and accept it.
  9. Statement of Net Liabilities. This shows the balance for last year plus preliminary tax for this year. Decide your preliminary tax option (Part 3).
  10. Pay and submit together on ROS before the ROS deadline. Save the PDF of the return and the payment receipt.

Part 10If things go wrong

Almost every tax problem gets smaller if you act early and get worse if you wait. File on time even if you can't pay, answer every Revenue letter by its deadline, and fix mistakes before Revenue finds them.

Situation What to do
You can't pay the full bill File on time anyway, then apply on ROS for a phased payment arrangement. Interest still runs, but you avoid the late filing surcharge and keep your tax clearance
You missed the deadline File as soon as possible. Within two months of the deadline the surcharge is 5% of the tax; after that it is 10% (Part 3)
You found a mistake in a filed return Amend the return on ROS, or make an unprompted qualifying disclosure if it is significant (Part 6)
A Revenue letter asks you to review something Treat it seriously: check your records, reply by the date given, correct any error. Ask an accountant if unsure
Revenue notifies an audit You can still make a (prompted) qualifying disclosure before the audit starts, which reduces penalties. Get an accountant involved now
You disagree with a Revenue assessment You can appeal to the Tax Appeals Commission, normally within 30 days of the assessment
You had a loss year The loss can be carried forward and set against future taxi profits, or set against other income in the same year
Your tax clearance is refused Find out what is outstanding (a missing return or unpaid balance), fix it, then reapply at least 48 hours before booking your SPSV renewal
You stop driving the taxi Tell Revenue you have ceased (form TRCN1 or the ROS cease-registration screen), file a final return, and account for any balancing allowance or charge on the car

What a taxi audit usually looks at

  • Whether declared takings match app reports, card settlements and bank lodgements
  • Whether cash takings look realistic for the hours you work
  • The business-use percentage of the car and how you worked it out
  • Receipts behind the biggest expense lines
  • The capital allowance working on the taxi

If you follow the workflow in Part 7, you will have an answer and a document for every one of these.

Where to get help

  • Revenue: MyEnquiries in myAccount or ROS, and revenue.ie
  • An accountant or tax adviser (Chartered Accountants Ireland, CPA Ireland or the Irish Tax Institute have member directories)
  • Citizens Information for free general guidance on tax and social welfare

Plain-English glossary

Term Meaning
Balancing allowance / charge The one-off adjustment when you sell the taxi for less / more than its tax written-down value
Capital allowance (wear and tear) The yearly tax write-off for the taxi and equipment, instead of depreciation
Chargeable person Someone who must file a self-assessment return (every self-employed driver)
DAC7 EU rules making taxi and other apps report drivers' earnings to Revenue each January
Earned Income Tax Credit The self-employed person's version of the PAYE credit
eTax Clearance Revenue's confirmation that your tax affairs are in order; needed for SPSV renewal
Form 11 The yearly income tax return for self-employed people
myAccount / ROS Revenue's online services; ROS is where self-employed people file and pay
Net relevant earnings Your taxi profit, used to set your pension relief limit
Pay and File The single yearly deadline to file last year's return and pay tax
Preliminary tax A down-payment on this year's tax, paid by 31 October (or the ROS date)

SPSV Small Public Service Vehicle: taxis, hackneys and limousines licensed by the NTA
